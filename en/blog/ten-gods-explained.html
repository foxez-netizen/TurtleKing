The Ten Gods in Saju Explained: Friend, Wealth, Officer, Resource and Output

After the Five Elements, the next layer of the Four Pillars is the Ten Gods. The name sounds mystical, but the idea is practical: it is a relationship labeling system. Every other character in your chart is named according to how it relates to you, meaning your Day Master. In Korean the system is called Sipseong (십성, 十星, "ten stars") and in Chinese Shishen (十神, "ten gods"). The word "god" here means something closer to "spirit" or "function."

The logic: five relationships, two polarities

Start from the Five Elements cycles. Compared with your Day Master, any other element falls into exactly one of five relationships:

  1. Same element as you: the "Peer" family (companions, rivals).
  2. The element you generate: the "Output" family (expression, talent).
  3. The element you control: the "Wealth" family (resources, results).
  4. The element that controls you: the "Officer" family (rules, authority, pressure).
  5. The element that generates you: the "Resource" family (support, learning, protection).

Each family is then split in two by polarity. If the other character has the same Yin/Yang polarity as your Day Master it is the "indirect" or "biased" version (pian, 偏); if the polarity is opposite it is the "direct" or "proper" version (zheng, 正). Two names are special: in the Peer family the same-polarity character is "Friend" and the opposite one is "Rob Wealth," and in the Output family the same-polarity character is "Eating God" and the opposite is "Hurting Officer." That gives ten.

What each family traditionally signifies

Peer: Friend and Rob Wealth

Peers are people and forces like you. In moderation they mean independence, self-confidence and good teammates; in excess they suggest competition for the same resources. Rob Wealth is the more aggressive version, associated with rivalry, risk-taking and sharing (or losing) money.

Output: Eating God and Hurting Officer

Output is how you express yourself. Eating God is gentle, appetite-and-enjoyment energy: craft, cooking, steady creativity. Hurting Officer is sharper and more rebellious: wit, critique, performance, a dislike of rigid rules. The name sounds negative, but it is among the most commonly cited energies in the charts of artists, comedians and innovators. A famous Korean idiom pair is "Sanggwan-paein," a Hurting Officer controlled by a Resource, which describes a sharp talent tempered by learning.

Wealth: Indirect and Direct

Wealth is what you can "control" or manage: income, assets, and in traditional readings the spouse for men. Direct Wealth is steady salary and careful saving; Indirect Wealth is variable income, trade, entrepreneurial or social money. Modern practitioners generally avoid the old spouse rules.

Officer: Direct Officer and Seven Killings

Officer is what controls you, and therefore authority, rules, career and responsibility. Direct Officer is orderly structure, reputation and institutions. Seven Killings is intense pressure and discipline: leadership under stress, the military, competitive fields. The name is dramatic but not a curse; the tradition says a well-balanced Seven Killings is the mark of a strong executive.

Resource: Direct and Indirect

Resource generates you: mentors, parents, education, shelter, credentials. Direct Resource is conventional learning and protection. Indirect Resource is unconventional knowledge, intuition, side interests and specialty skills.

Strong or weak Day Master?

A major use of Ten Gods is to judge whether the Day Master is strong (supported by Peers and Resources, especially in the birth season) or weak (surrounded by Output, Wealth and Officer that drain or restrain it). A strong chart tends to benefit from outlets, goals and challenges; a weak chart tends to benefit from support and peers. Practitioners call the favorable element the "useful god" (yongshin, 用神). Methods for choosing it differ widely, and this is where two experienced readers most often disagree. Our calculator provides a rough strength estimate, but we treat that as a starting point.

Ten Gods in branches

Branches have Ten Gods too, but they are usually computed from the hidden stems inside each branch, most importantly the main hidden stem. For a Day Master of Geng (庚, Yang Metal), the branch Wu (午, Horse) has main hidden stem Ding (丁), which is Yin Fire and controls Metal with opposite polarity, so it reads as Direct Officer. That matches the result in our walkthrough chart.

Cautions when you meet the Ten Gods online

  • Names are not verdicts. "Seven Killings" and "Hurting Officer" are technical labels. Lists that claim "people with this god are unfaithful/violent/lucky" are generalizations that the classical texts do not support so flatly.
  • Position and number matter. A single Hurting Officer in the hour pillar is read very differently from four of them in the month and day.
  • Context matters. Gods are interpreted together with element balance, season, branch interactions and the current luck cycle.

The Ten Gods are best treated as a language for asking better questions: What do I do with my energy? Who supports me? Where do I feel pressure? If a description rings true, use it; if not, set it down.
