Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches: The 60-Year Cycle Behind Saju and the Chinese Zodiac

Guide · 5 min read · Published 2026-10-08 · By Lucky Turtle Saju

The ten Heavenly StemsThe twelve Earthly BranchesHow 10 and 12 make 60Which year is which?Hidden stems: the layer beneath each branchA five-minute exercise

Every chart in the Four Pillars of Destiny is built from just two alphabets: ten Heavenly Stems and twelve Earthly Branches. Together they form the sexagenary cycle, a 60-step counting system used in China, Korea, Japan and Vietnam for more than two thousand years. Once you know these 22 characters, a Saju chart stops looking like a wall of symbols and starts to look like a grid you can read.

The ten Heavenly Stems

The Heavenly Stems (天干, cheongan in Korean) pair up into five elements, each in a Yang and a Yin form. The order below is the fixed order of the cycle and should be memorized as a sequence.

A useful shortcut: odd-numbered stems are Yang and even-numbered stems are Yin, and every two consecutive stems share an element. The Five Elements guide gives the traditional image attached to each one.

The twelve Earthly Branches

The Earthly Branches (地支, jiji) double as the twelve animals of the zodiac, the twelve two-hour periods of the day, and the twelve months of the solar year.

Two points trip up almost every beginner. First, the month column is based on solar terms, not on the calendar month: the "Tiger month" starts around February 4, not January 1. Second, the Rat hour starts at 23:00, an hour before midnight. Whether a baby born between 23:00 and midnight belongs to the previous or next day is a long-running debate that we cover in the Korean versus Chinese comparison.

Why the odd dates? The solar terms are defined by the Sun's position, so the exact day varies by a day or so from year to year. For instance, Lichun fell on February 4 at about 04:58 Korean time in 2026. In Saju the exact minute can matter if you were born near a boundary.

How 10 and 12 make 60

To label a year, month, day or hour, the cycle pairs the first stem with the first branch, the second with the second, and so on, running both lists in parallel. After 10 steps the stems start over while the branches are still on their tenth entry, so the 11th pair is Jia with Xu (甲戌), the 12th is Yi with Hai (乙亥), and so on. Because 10 and 12 share a common factor of 2, only half of the 120 possible pairs ever occur: Yang stems only combine with Yang branches (the odd-numbered ones) and Yin stems only with Yin branches. The cycle repeats after 60 steps, when Jia and Zi meet again.

Each year and day pillar is one of these 60 pairs, while month and hour pillars have only 12 possible branches whose stems are fixed by the year stem and the day stem respectively. That gives at most 60 × 12 × 60 × 12 = 518,400 distinct charts. With roughly eight billion people alive, any given chart is shared, on average, by on the order of ten thousand people, which is one reason serious practitioners emphasize context, the luck cycles and the person in front of them over the chart alone.

Which year is which?

A year's pillar is found by counting. 1984 was Jia-Zi (甲子), the start of a cycle. 1990 was Geng-Wu (庚午), the Metal Horse year. 2026 is Bing-Wu (丙午), the Fire Horse year, and 2027 will be Ding-Wei (丁未), the Fire Goat. The next Jia-Zi year is 2044.

Simple rule for the stem: take the last digit of the year; 4 is Jia, 5 is Yi, 6 is Bing, 7 is Ding, 8 is Wu, 9 is Ji, 0 is Geng, 1 is Xin, 2 is Ren, 3 is Gui. So any year ending in 6 begins with a Fire Yang stem. For the branch, take the year modulo 12: 4 is Rat, 5 is Ox, 6 is Tiger, and so on.

Remember that the traditional year changes at Lichun. A person born on February 1 belongs to the previous year's pillar for Saju purposes, even though a Lunar New Year zodiac might already have switched. See the zodiac-year post for a real example.

Hidden stems: the layer beneath each branch

Each Earthly Branch is considered to contain one to three Heavenly Stems that represent "hidden" influences. For example, Yin (寅, Tiger) contains Jia (Wood), Bing (Fire) and Wu (Earth): the tiger month begins in the cold of early spring but carries the warmth that will come. Hidden stems are why some practitioners count a chart's elements beyond the eight visible characters, and they are used for the Ten Gods analysis of branches. We explain that in the Ten Gods guide.

A five-minute exercise

To check you have absorbed the system, try this. Look up your birth year pillar, then identify its stem's element and polarity (is it Yang Fire, Yin Water?) and its branch's animal. Next, compare that with your zodiac animal if you celebrate Lunar New Year: if you were born in January or early February, the two may disagree.
