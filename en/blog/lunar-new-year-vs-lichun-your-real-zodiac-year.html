Lunar New Year vs Lichun: What Is Your Real Chinese Zodiac Year in Saju?

"What's your zodiac sign?" has two common answers in East Asia, and they do not always agree. The popular answer follows the lunar New Year: if you were born before it, you belong to the previous animal. The Four Pillars answer follows Lichun (立春, "Start of Spring"), a solar term that falls around February 4. For anyone born in January or early February, those two rules can give different animals. This post explains why, with a worked example, and what it means for your Saju chart.

Two calendars, two new years

Lunar New Year falls on the second new moon after the winter solstice (broadly speaking), so it moves between about January 21 and February 20 on the Gregorian calendar. It is a cultural and family holiday celebrated across East and Southeast Asia, and it is the boundary most people use for the zodiac animal.

Lichun is a solar term, defined by the Sun reaching 315 degrees of ecliptic longitude, which happens around February 3 to 5 every year. Saju, a solar system, starts its year there. In 2026, Lichun arrived on February 4 at about 04:58 Korean time. The date varies by hours between years, but it stays within a narrow window, unlike the Lunar New Year.

A worked example

Consider someone born on February 1, 1990.

  • The Lunar New Year of 1990 was January 27. By the popular rule, this person is in the year of the Horse (庚午, Geng-Wu), since the new year had already begun.
  • Lichun of 1990 was on February 4. By the Saju rule, February 1 is still before the start of the solar year, so this person belongs to the previous year's pillar, Ji-Si (己巳), the Snake.

Our calculator confirms this: February 1, 1990 returns the year pillar Ji-Si, while February 5, 1990 returns Geng-Wu. The same person can say "I'm a Horse" at a family party and be a Snake in their Saju chart. Both are right within their own system.

So there is a window of a week or more, between Lunar New Year and Lichun, in some years, when the two animals disagree. In other years the Lunar New Year falls after Lichun (for example, when it arrives in mid-February), and the window is much wider: everyone born between Lichun and the Lunar New Year switches in the opposite direction.

Why does Saju use Lichun?

The Four Pillars treat the year as a cycle of the Sun's energy. The solar year in the Chinese tradition begins when spring begins, the point midway between the winter solstice and the spring equinox. That is a sensible astronomical anchor, and it keeps months aligned with the seasons: the Tiger month starts at Lichun, the Rabbit month at Jingzhe, and so on. Using the Sun means the chart does not wander with the moon's irregular relationship to the seasons.

Not everyone agrees on edge cases. Some modern teachers argue the year should turn at the winter solstice or the Lunar New Year, and in popular Korean culture many people use Lunar New Year or even January 1 for the "zodiac." But within classical Saju practice, and in all mainstream Bazi and saju software, Lichun is the rule.

Months have the same issue

Just as the year changes at Lichun, each month changes at a solar-term boundary, not on the first of the Gregorian month. The twelve "jie" boundaries fall around the 4th to 8th of each month:

For instance, someone born on May 3, 1990 falls in the Dragon month (Geng-Chen, 庚辰), because the Snake month did not begin until May 6, while a person born on May 6 has Xin-Si (辛巳). That affects not just the month animal but also the element balance and the luck cycles, which are derived from the month pillar.

What if you were born near the boundary?

If you were born within a day or two of a solar term, the exact time matters. Lichun in 2026 happened at 04:58 on February 4. A baby born at 03:00 that morning belongs to the previous year's pillar; one born at 06:00 belongs to the new year. Because the calculation uses the Sun's actual position, the time of the term differs from year to year by several hours.

In these cases:

  1. Confirm your birth time as precisely as you can.
  2. Confirm that the calculator uses the correct time zone for your birth; see the Korean versus Chinese comparison.
  3. Compare with a published table of solar terms, such as the one from the Korea Astronomy and Space Science Institute.

And your Western zodiac sign?

Western signs are a third system again: they follow the Sun's tropical longitude, starting with Aries on the March equinox. Your Western sign, your popular Chinese zodiac animal, and your Saju year and month animals can all differ. It is perfectly consistent to be a Pisces, a Horse and a Snake at the same time. We compare the Western and Saju frameworks in this guide.

The short version

  • The popular zodiac turns at Lunar New Year; the Saju year turns at Lichun (around February 4).
  • If you were born in January or early February, check both.
  • Saju months turn at solar terms around the 4th to 8th, not on the 1st.
  • For an accurate chart, use a calculator that computes solar terms, and confirm your result if you were born near a boundary.
