Saju Luck Cycles Explained: 10-Year Daeun and Yearly Seun Pillars

Guide · 5 min read · Published 2026-10-08 · By Lucky Turtle Saju

A natal Four Pillars chart is fixed at birth, but life is not. To account for change over time, Saju adds two moving layers: the 10-year luck cycles (Daeun in Korean, Da Yun 大運 in Chinese) and the annual pillar (Seun 세운, Liu Nian 流年). Think of the natal chart as a landscape and the luck cycles as the weather that passes over it, with decades-long climate shifts and year-by-year conditions.

What a 10-year luck cycle is

A luck cycle is simply another stem-and-branch pair, like a pillar, that governs ten years of your life. A new cycle begins at a specific age, and then every ten years the next pair in the 60-cycle takes over. Because it is another pair of characters, it is interpreted exactly like the natal ones: what element is it, what Ten God is it relative to your Day Master, and how do its branches combine with or clash against the chart?

The first cycle does not start at birth. It starts a few years in, at an age called the Daeun start age, and the earlier childhood is governed by the month pillar itself.

Which direction do the cycles run?

The cycles are derived from your month pillar. They step either forward or backward through the 60-cycle starting from it, according to a traditional rule involving the polarity of your birth year stem and your gender:

Yang year stem (Jia, Bing, Wu, Geng, Ren)Yin year stem (Yi, Ding, Ji, Xin, Gui)
MaleForwardBackward
FemaleBackwardForward

In Korean this is often summarized as "yang-male and yin-female go forward." The rule is a convention of the tradition, based on Yin/Yang symmetry. Some modern practitioners question tying it to a binary gender, and a few simply choose the direction that fits the person. Our calculator asks for gender precisely because of this rule, and you should know that the choice alters the cycles shown.

Calculating the start age

The start age comes from the distance between your birth moment and a solar term:

  • If the cycles run forward, count the days from your birth to the next solar-term boundary (jie).
  • If they run backward, count the days from your birth back to the previous boundary.

Then divide by three: three days equal one year, so one day equals four months, and each two-hour block equals ten days. If the next boundary is 21 days away, the first cycle begins at 7 years. The rule is another traditional convention; its justification is symbolic (a three-day-to-a-year compression), not astronomical.

A worked example

Take a man born on May 15, 1990 at 14:30 in Seoul. His year stem Geng (庚) is Yang, and since he is male his cycles run forward from his month pillar Xin-Si (辛巳). The next solar term after his birth, Mangzhong, arrives about 22 days later, which our calculator converts to a start age of 7 years and 3 months. His cycle sequence then is:

Relative to a Geng Day Master, Jia is Indirect Wealth and Yi is Direct Wealth, so the tradition would describe his thirties as a wealth-oriented period with themes of money, practical results and the outside world, and his forties as a continuation with a more orderly flavor. These are the standard textbook readings, and a skilled reader would temper them with the whole chart.

The annual pillar

The yearly pillar is simply the stem and branch of the current year, and it changes at Lichun (early February), not on January 1 and not at Lunar New Year. It is layered over the current luck cycle. 2026 is Bing-Wu (丙午), the Fire Horse year; 2027 is Ding-Wei (丁未), the Fire Goat. To see which pillar your own birth year carries, read this post on zodiac years.

A reader looks at how the annual pillar relates to the natal chart. For example, an annual branch that clashes with your day branch (such as Horse against Rat) is traditionally associated with movement and disruption in the area of life that the day branch represents; a branch that combines with it is associated with connection. These are patterns of symbolism, which should be held lightly.

2026 is a Fire Horse year. You may see a lot of popular commentary about Fire Horse years, especially in Japan, where a superstition around the hinoe-uma year famously affected the 1966 birth rate. That is a cultural belief about a specific year, not a part of ordinary Four Pillars analysis.

Monthly and daily layers

Practitioners also look at monthly pillars (changing at solar terms) and daily pillars for timing smaller decisions. The shorter the time scale, the weaker the evidence, and most serious Saju readers focus on the cycle and the year.

How to use luck cycles sensibly

  • Use them as themes, not forecasts. A "wealth" cycle does not mean you will get rich. It suggests that questions of money, results and responsibility may be in the foreground.
  • Look at transitions. The years around a switch between cycles are traditionally seen as periods of change; it can be useful to ask yourself what you would like to change anyway.
  • Never let it make hard decisions for you. Do not delay medical care, quit a job, or end a relationship on the basis of a cycle.
  • Compare sources. Different schools place cycle boundaries and read gods differently. If two charts disagree, that tells you about the method rather than about your life.
