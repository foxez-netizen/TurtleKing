The Lucky Turtle Saju calculator (느릿느릿 사주풀이) is a free web tool that turns a birth date and time into a full Four Pillars chart. It is the engine behind this site's guides, including the worked-example walkthrough. Below is what it does, and what you should know before relying on it.

How it calculates

Many online calculators use lookup tables of solar-term dates copied from a printed almanac. This one computes them. According to the site's documentation, the tool calculates the Sun's ecliptic longitude using the Meeus algorithm with a perturbation correction, finds the moment the Sun reaches each 15-degree boundary, and builds pillars from those instants. Day pillars come from a Julian day count in the continuous 60-day cycle, and the lunar calendar is derived from astronomical new moons and the 24 solar terms. The calculation runs in your browser, and the site states that birth data is neither sent to nor stored on a server.

We think that is a good design choice for two reasons. It makes the tool work for any date in a very wide range, and it avoids transcription errors from tables. The tradeoff is that an algorithmic solar-term time can differ from the officially published time by a few minutes, which matters only if you were born in the minutes around a boundary.

How we checked it

We verified the arithmetic with spot checks against facts that are easy to confirm independently:

  • Year pillars match the 60-cycle: 1984 returns Jia-Zi, 1990 Geng-Wu, and 2026 Bing-Wu.
  • Lichun boundary for 2026 is computed as February 4, about 04:58 Korean time, which agrees with the date generally published for that year. A birth on February 1, 1990 returns the year pillar Ji-Si (the Snake), while February 5, 1990 returns Geng-Wu (the Horse), as the rules require.
  • Month pillars change at the solar-term boundaries: May 3, 1990 returns the Chen (Dragon) month and May 6 the Si (Snake) month.
  • Luck cycles follow the standard forward/backward rule and the three-days-per-year start-age convention.

This is a smoke test, not a certification. We recommend that anyone born near a solar-term boundary or a Zi-hour boundary confirm the result with the Korea Astronomy and Space Science Institute's published solar-term table or a second calculator.

What we like

  • Transparent method. The guide page explains how the calendar and solar terms are computed, rather than treating it as a black box.
  • Both Zi-hour conventions. Rare among free tools, and it matters for late-night births.
  • Hidden-stem weighting. The element bar chart includes hidden stems, which gives a more nuanced picture than a simple count of eight characters.
  • Privacy-friendly. No account, no upload,.
  • Honest framing. Results are presented as a traditional reference interpretation, not a prediction.

Where it falls short

  • Korean-only interface. This is the largest limitation for readers of this blog. The labels are in Korean and Hanja. That is why we wrote the step-by-step guide that translates each field. An English version of the calculator is the most obvious improvement we could make.
  • No daylight-saving or historical time-zone adjustments. The tool treats the entered time as standard Korean time. If you were born during Korea's daylight saving years or the 1954–1961 UTC+8:30 period, convert your birth time first.
  • Single school of interpretation. The strength rating is a heuristic score. Deeper judgments such as the "useful god" depend on a practitioner's school and are not automated.
  • Historical lunar dates. The site's own guide notes that lunar dates in the 1950s time-zone-change period may differ by a day from the official almanac.
  • Limited narrative. The tool gives structured data and brief explanations rather than a personalized essay. For some users that is a feature, and for others a gap.

Verdict

For a free tool, the Lucky Turtle calculator is accurate, transparent and respectful of privacy, and well suited to learning how a chart is built. Treat its interpretation as a starting point, double-check births near a boundary, and do not use any output as a basis for major life decisions. If you are an English speaker, read the walkthrough first, then try your own date.
