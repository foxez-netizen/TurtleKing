How to Read Your Saju Chart Step by Step (With a Worked Example)

Reading a Four Pillars chart is easier once you have seen it done. This walkthrough takes one sample birth, computes the chart with our free Saju calculator, and reads it in seven steps. The calculator's interface is currently in Korean, so we list the labels you will meet along the way.

Sample person: a man born on May 15, 1990 at 14:30 in Seoul, solar calendar. Optional settings were left at their defaults, meaning no Seoul longitude correction. The numbers below were produced by the same calculation engine that powers the calculator.

Step 1. Enter the birth data

The form has four parts:

  • Birth date (생년월일): choose Solar (양력) or Lunar (음력). If you pick lunar, a leap month (윤달) checkbox appears. Use solar if you are unsure; the standard Gregorian date on your birth certificate is solar.
  • Birth time (태어난 시각), Korean time: or tick "I don't know my birth time" (태어난 시각을 몰라요) to build a three-pillar chart.
  • Gender (성별): Male (남성) or Female (여성). It is used only to decide the direction of the luck cycles.
  • Advanced settings (고급 설정): how to handle births between 23:00 and midnight (야자시 keeps the current day; 조자시 moves to the next day), and an optional Seoul longitude correction of minus 30 minutes. See our Korean versus Chinese comparison for why these exist.

Press the View Saju (사주 보기) button. Birth data is processed in your browser; the site states that it is not sent to or stored on a server.

Saju charts are traditionally written right to left, with the year on the right and the hour on the left, but many Western materials show them left to right. Our result screen follows the Korean convention.

The year pillar is Geng-Wu (庚午), the Metal Horse. The month pillar Xin-Si (辛巳) begins at the solar term Lixia in early May. This person was born ten days after it, so the month branch is Si, the Snake, not the Dragon month that ordinary "May" might suggest.

Step 3. Identify the Day Master

The Day Master is the top character of the day pillar, here Geng (庚), Yang Metal. Traditionally pictured as raw iron or an axe, it is direct, firm and strongly shaped by pressure. Which keyword feels right will vary; the mnemonic is a starting point only. The day pillar Geng-Chen (庚辰) also happens to be one of the symbolic-star patterns called Gwegang (魁罡), traditionally associated with strong will.

Step 4. Count the Five Elements

Counting the eight visible characters gives:

WoodFireEarthMetalWater
02 (Si, Wu)2 (Chen, Wei)3 (Geng, Xin, Geng)1 (Gui)

This chart has no visible Wood, three Metal (the Day Master's own element, strengthened by Earth under it), and a pair of Fire in the month and year. Weighted counts, which include the hidden stems, produce a more nuanced picture: roughly Wood 0.4, Fire 1.9, Earth 1.6, Metal 3.2, Water 1.2. So Wood is not entirely absent; a small amount is hiding in the branches (for example inside Chen and Wei). See the Five Elements guide for the cycles.

Seasonally, the person was born in early summer (the Si month), when Fire is strong and Metal is traditionally considered weakened or "in the fire." Even though Metal is abundant, the calculator therefore rates the Day Master as roughly balanced, leaning weak, and not "in season." In practice this tends to be read as: the chart would benefit from the supporting elements Earth and Metal and from Water to cool the heat.

Even without further analysis, you can see a theme: two kinds of Officer (the month and year branches, Fire controlling Metal), two kinds of Resource (the Earth beneath the day and hour), and one Hurting Officer in the hour. A traditional reading would say this chart has plenty of pressure and authority around it, supported by Earth and learning, with a talent for expression emerging later in life. That is a description of a pattern, and many readers would phrase it differently.

Step 6. Look for interactions and stars

The calculator also checks interactions between branches. In this chart it finds a six-combination between the year branch Wu (午) and the hour branch Wei (未), which traditionally "combines toward Fire." A combination can be read as an attachment or link between those two pillars, here between ancestry and one's later years or children.

Symbolic stars (sinsal in Korean) detected include Tianyi Noble (Cheoneul Gwiin, 天乙貴人) in the hour pillar, Gwegang in the day pillar, and the Heavenly Virtue and Monthly Virtue nobles in the stems. Names like "Noble" sound auspicious; others in the same family (Peach Blossom, White Tiger) sound ominous. The tradition says stars are secondary clues and should never outweigh the structure of the chart.

Step 7. Check the luck cycles

Because this man was born in a Yang year (Geng) and is male, his luck cycles run forward from the month pillar. They start at about age 7 and 3 months: Ren-Wu (1997–2006), Gui-Wei (2007–2016), Jia-Shen (2017–2026) and Yi-You from 2027. The current year, 2026, is Bing-Wu (丙午), a Fire Horse year, which relates to this chart's year branch (also Wu) and strengthens the Fire already present. A traditional reader would flag it as an energetic year with attention on authority and pressure, and might suggest rest and cooling influences.

Putting it together

A short summary, in the cautious language a good reader would use:

The chart is a Yang Metal Day Master born in early summer, with a lot of Metal and Earth but pressure from Fire. Key themes are structure, responsibility, and the need to find balance between holding firm and cooling down. The luck cycle through 2026 emphasizes practical results, and the one after it, starting in 2027, emphasizes steadiness.

Notice what the summary does not say: it makes no claim about marriage, wealth, longevity or health. A responsible reading stays at the level of tendencies and themes, and acknowledges that the same chart could belong to thousands of very different people.

Tips for your own chart

  • Check your birth time first. Charts built on a guessed time can change the hour pillar and even the day pillar near midnight.
  • If you were born within a day of a solar term (early each month), verify the time of the term against an official almanac. In Korea, the Korea Astronomy and Space Science Institute publishes them.
  • Compare two calculators. If they disagree on the month or day pillar, find out whether they use different time zone, midnight or longitude settings.
  • Use the chart as one reflective prompt among many.
